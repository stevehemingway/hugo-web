# Carvana (stocks/CVNA)

Thesis: economics, analyst: kerrisdale-capital, sector: retail.

<blockquote>
<pre>
Kerrisdale Capital published a follow-up report on Carvana (NYSE: CVNA — $16.8 billion), an online platform for buying and selling used cars. Kerrisdale raised concerns about the company's valuation and challenging growth prospects and said the company is "a poorly capitalized, growth-challenged auto retailer now valued at an absurd 42x EV/2024E EBITDA." Kerrisdale also published a letter to Carvana's auditor, Grant Thornton, raising concerns about its auto financing agreements with DriveTime Automotive. Kerrisdale wrote, in part,

    "Carvana's relationship with DriveTime is far from arm's length, in our opinion. DriveTime is controlled by Ernie Garcia II, a controlling shareholder of Carvana and father of Carvana's CEO Ernie Garcia III, making them literally related parties."
</pre>
</blockquote>

**Recent Financial Performance (Q4 2024 / FY2024)**

Carvana delivered a stunning turnaround in 2024, reporting its first profitable year with record results:

| Metric | Q4 2024 | FY2024 |
|--------|---------|--------|
| Revenue | $3.55B (+46% YoY) | $13.67B |
| Retail Units Sold | 114,379 (+50% YoY) | ~460,000 |
| Net Income | Significant profit | $404M |
| Adjusted EBITDA | Strong margin | $1.38B (10.1% margin) |

Key highlights from the February 19, 2025 earnings call:
- **Gross profit per unit (GPU)** reached $7,200, up dramatically from the lows of 2022-2023
- Management emphasized the company's "significant growth" potential for 2025
- Operating leverage demonstrated as revenue growth outpaced cost growth
- Cash generation improved, allowing debt reduction

**Competitive Position**

Carvana operates in the highly fragmented U.S. used car market (~$800B+ TAM) with only ~1% market share:

- **vs. CarMax**: CarMax remains the largest used car retailer by volume but growth has stagnated. Carvana now leads in inventory turnover (2.14x vs. CarMax's ~1.8x) and gross margins (19.3%).
- **vs. Vroom/Shift**: Both online competitors have either pivoted away from retail or ceased operations entirely, leaving Carvana as the primary pure-play online used car retailer.
- **vs. AutoNation**: The traditional dealer group continues to compete but lacks Carvana's digital-first infrastructure and national reach.

Carvana's competitive advantages include its proprietary vending machine infrastructure (lower variable costs at scale), AI-driven pricing, and seamless online purchase experience. However, the moat remains questionable—traditional dealers and CarMax continue to invest heavily in their digital platforms.

**Debt, Interest Rate Risk & Economic Downturn Survival**

Despite the turnaround, Carvana's balance sheet remains a significant vulnerability:

- **Total debt**: Reduced from a peak of $8.8B to approximately $5.6B (late 2025), but leverage remains high
- **Cash interest obligations**: Starting February 2025, Carvana must pay ~$215M annually in cash interest on $2.4B of long-term debt
- **Debt structure**: Much of the debt is high-yield with rates reflecting the company's risky profile

**Recession/Interest Rate Risks:**
1. **Auto financing sensitivity**: Carvana relies heavily on third-party financing for customers. Higher interest rates reduce affordability and can significantly impact demand. The company noted on the earnings call that they are monitoring consumer credit conditions closely.

2. **GPU compression risk**: The $7,200 GPU is historically elevated. In a downturn, used car prices typically fall, and financing spreads compress. Management acknowledged on the call that GPU has "pushes and pulls" but expects stability—however, this assumes a stable macro environment.

3. **Fixed cost structure**: The vending machine network and logistics infrastructure carry high fixed costs. A sustained volume decline (as seen in 2022-2023 when units dropped ~20%) would quickly erode profitability.

4. **Related-party financing concerns**: The Kerrisdale report highlighted Carvana's reliance on DriveTime (controlled by CEO Ernie Garcia's father) for financing. This relationship adds governance risk and potential conflicts of interest.

**Survival Assessment**: Carvana survived the 2022-2023 near-death experience by cutting costs aggressively and restructuring debt. However, the company remains vulnerable to a severe recession or sustained high interest rates. The current valuation (trading at ~$400/share vs. ~$5 in 2023) appears to price in flawless execution and a soft landing scenario.

**Earnings Call Commentary (Feb 19, 2025)**

CEO Ernie Garcia expressed confidence in continued growth, stating the company had a "full year" of profitability and emphasizing that they see "significant growth" ahead for 2025. When pressed by analysts on GPU sustainability, Garcia deflected specifics but noted the company has multiple levers to pull. CFO Mark Jenkins highlighted the improved cash generation and debt reduction trajectory. The tone was notably optimistic compared to the defensive posture of prior years.

**Investment Conclusion**

Carvana's operational turnaround is genuine—cost discipline and operational efficiency have dramatically improved. However, at ~40x+ EV/EBITDA and with significant debt maturities ahead, the stock appears to be pricing in a best-case scenario. The combination of high leverage, interest rate sensitivity, and cyclical auto exposure makes this a risky short candidate for those who believe a recession or credit tightening is imminent. The short thesis rests on: (1) GPU normalization in a weaker economy, (2) debt refinancing risks, and (3) governance concerns around related-party transactions.
