## Bear case on Csquare Inc (NYSE: CSQR)

There isn't a classic screaming short-seller deck floating around publicly, but there is one live bear campaign and a stack of solid structural bear arguments. Summary of what I found:

**The active one: DF Research (Oct 21-22, 2026)**
- Announced a report on CSQR, published Thursday (yesterday).
- Core thesis: "a debt-laden portfolio of legacy data centers" facing "substantial cash deficits in 2026."
- This is the most direct bear voice on the name right now. I could not pull the full PDF from search results — TipRanks/The Fly only carried the announcement line. If you want the actual report, it's probably behind DF Research's own distribution (often emailed or posted to their site/substack).

**Short interest is climbing fast**
- 30 Sep 2026: 4,705,175 shares short, up **139.3%** from 1,966,192 on 15 Sep (which was itself +46.9% from 31 Aug).
- Sharemaestro flagged a "Crowded Short" setup score of 89/100, shorts +139.3%, price -2.4% over the report interval, days to cover 1.95.
- Absolute short % of float still modest (~2.8% per Defense World, 0.8% per MarketBeat's older float measure) — so this is a fast-building position, not yet a crowded consensus short.

**The structural bear arguments (widely repeated)**
- **Leverage:** ~$5.76B total debt against ~$120M cash. Q2 interest expense $92.8M on ~$4.89B long-term debt. Debt is more than double the ~$2.83B market cap at one point.
- **Cash flow:** negative ~$570M free cash flow from ~$140M operating cash flow. Interest expense growth (+62-71%) outpacing revenue/EBITDA growth.
- **Earnings quality:** Q2 revenue $280.4M (+14.5%), adj EBITDA $120.3M (+21%), but **net loss $48.8M** vs $13.9M a year earlier. Q1 was a $66M loss on $270.5M revenue.
- **IPO reception:** priced below range at $21, weak debut; currently trades ~21-22% below IPO, near 52-week low ($15.52).
- **Legacy asset mix:** bear framing is that this is Brookfield's legacy colocation portfolio dressed as AI infrastructure — 64-80 data centers, carrier-neutral, but not the 150kW/rack AI-density story bulls claim.
- **Contract renewal cliff:** significant portion of contracts expiring 2026-2027; customer concentration risk (Trefis).
- **Competition:** Equinix and Digital Realty have cheaper capital and denser interconnection; hyperscalers can insource workloads.
- **Lockup/supply overhang:** roughly a third of the company is held by pre-IPO holders; lockup expiry is a supply event.
- **Insider optics:** execs/directors received ~$97M in equity grants at $21 shortly after IPO; stock now well below that (Basis Report).

**The other side, for balance**
- Consensus is Buy, 10 analysts, average PT $28.40 (+68%); low $24, high $46.
- Bulls point to 107% utilization, surging bookings, 1,700+ clients with low churn, and EBITDA growth at a discount to Equinix.

**My read:** the bear case is not an accounting-fraud allegation — it's a leverage-and-cash-flow story. That's harder to rebut with a single datapoint and easier for shorts to keep pressing. The +139% short interest jump two weeks before DF Research went public suggests positioning was already building ahead of the report.
