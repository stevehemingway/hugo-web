# The Military Industrial Complex
Eisenhower warned against the rise of the power of the defence industry, aided and abetted by the military, who consume their products.

Sadly, nobody listened to him, and a huge part of US GDP in the 50s, 60s and 70s was spend on defence, both because of the wars in Korea, Vietnam and because of the Apollo program (and various programs connected to the Cold War).

There was a peace dividend in the early 2000s when Bill Clinton managed to get the Federal budget into surplus, briefly, but this was quickly terminated by 'forever wars' in Iraq, Afghanistan, Syria and, now, Ukraine.

I don't know or care much about the politics, but I know that this sort of government spending makes a difference to the market.
The go-go stocks in the 60s were defence contractors.
The competitive position is fantastic: no Chinese missile maker is going to elbow Lockheed Martin out of a contract with the Pentagon, no matter how much better, and cheaper, their hardware is.
These programs are huge, and run for decades. 
There is no possibility of governments negotiating watertight contracts to control costs.
